# Placement

The placement engine (`src/lib/placement/`) works out where a team stands on one map: five heroes, the phantimal and the artifact, on the map's ally tiles. It scores a placement by what each unit's skill does from where it stands when the battle starts, read from the same skill code the boards draw, and by how the heroes stand in recorded formations. The PvP team generator draws a board in its formation where the board's team has no recorded game on the map, and on every other board when the board's switch asks for it. On a map with no recorded games yet, it also scales a borrowed team's odds by the share of its goals' points the engine's best placement keeps there, against the map the team was recorded on (`effectPoints`, `src/lib/pvp/sl/generator/fit.ts`).

Two things make it hard. A team has too many placements to score them all on a page: five heroes on 13 tiles are 154,440 placements, and a phantimal makes eight times as many. And most goals leave many placements as good as each other, so the engine has to say how sure it is as well as pick one.

## What it takes

`placeTeam(team, mapKey, data, lookups)` (`engine.ts`) returns the placement it picks, the best score found, how many placements were about as good and the tiles each hero stands on among them. Three inputs come from outside the engine:

| Input     | From                                                                                                                | Holds                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rules     | `rules` in each hero's, phantimal's, artifact's and charm's data file (`src/lib/types/rule.ts`)                     | What the engine wants from one effect: who it should land on, or what it should reach. A unit can have one rule for its own team and one against the other.                                   |
| `data`    | `placement` in the Supreme League data file, written by the external report generator (`PlacementData`, `types.ts`) | The lists the rules name, the heroes who lean to the front or the back, rules found in recorded formations, the weights, and how often each enemy hero with a rule against us is on each map. |
| `lookups` | `PLACEMENT_LOOKUPS` (`lookups.ts`), over the data files                                                             | Each unit's faction, class and rules, each hero's charm and each artifact's rules.                                                                                                            |

The engine imports from `src/lib` alone, and its lookups from the data loader, so its tests run it without a page. It reads each map's tiles and buff tiles from the map files (`src/lib/maps.ts`), never from `data`.

## The board

`PlacementBoard` (`board.ts`) holds one team on one map in a `Grid` with its own `SkillManager`. It places each unit once with `executePlaceCharacter`, so each skill spawns its companions, and from then on lifts the units that move and puts them down with the low-level `performPlace`, every skill updated once after the move, as a board does after a drag. The units are the heroes in the team's order, then the phantimal and the companions skills spawned, so a companion with a tile of its own, such as Lailah, is placed like a hero.

## Scoring

`PlacementScorer` (`score.ts`) scores the board as it stands. Each rule reads where its effect lands from the unit's skill: the skill's target and arrows, and the tiles it marks (`getMarkedTiles`, [Skills](./SKILLS.md)). A rule for an effect Stargazer draws no skill for, every charm's and Ludovic's everbloom field, names its tile instead: the ally behind or in front of its holder, or the tiles around it. An artifact's rule reads the artifact's arrows (`reach.ts`). A companion counts as its hero and a phantimal's companion as its phantimal, except for `exactly`, where each counts as a unit of its own, and the effect's holder never counts for itself. A phantimal and each of its companions give a Spirit Mark of their own, so the Wedges' effect is on two even where one mark finds no hero.

| Goal                     | Met                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `target`                 | The effect lands on the team's first hero on the list. An effect on two wants the first two, half each. A team with none of the list's heroes falls back on the `damage` list.                                       |
| `include`                | A share for each of the team's listed heroes the effect reaches. An effect on one unit needs only one of them.                                                                                                       |
| `avoid`                  | The effect misses the team's first hero on the list.                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `atMost`, `exactly`      | The count of units on the marked tiles, with `sameFaction` one faction among them, and with `nonHero` the share of the goal met when a unit other than the team's heroes, a phantimal or a companion, is among them. |
| `most`, `{most:'walls'}` | The enemy tiles, or the walls on the enemy's side, among the marked tiles, as a share of the most the holder can mark from any of its tiles on that map, worked out on a board of its own.                           |
| `{most:'allies'}`        | The team's units on the marked tiles, as a share of the most ally tiles the holder can mark from any of its tiles, at most the rest of the team.                                                                     |

A placement's score has three parts, compared in this order. `broken` counts the rules from recorded formations marked `always` that it breaks. `goals` adds each rule's weight times how much of its goal is met, and the buff weight for each hero on the map's ally buff tile. `total` adds to the goals the light preferences, times the `lean` weight: how far toward the side it leans each hero stands, and each `usually` rule that holds. A season whose buff tiles stood elsewhere scores none (`buffTiles: false`).

A rule against us is read with its enemy unit alone on the enemy's side, tried on each enemy tile in turn, every tile as likely, so a skill that reads its own teammates, such as Reinier's, finds none. Its points are its weight times the share of those tiles where its goal is met, times how often the unit is on the map (`threats()`). They choose only among placements equally good on the team's own score, since a team's own goals come first and trying each enemy unit on every enemy tile costs several times as much as the rest of a score. The engine weighs a rule against us only for the heroes the data file gives a pick rate on the map, so a rule against us added to a hero counts once the file is written again with that hero in it.

## Search and choice

The search climbs from 32 placements drawn at random, seeded from the map and the team so a rebuild gives the same placement. Each climb takes the best step until none improves, a step being one unit moved to a free tile or two units trading tiles, and then the best move of two units together, each to a free tile or to the other's, since some goals need two units to move at once, such as Saida's two seeds. A goal that needs three units to move together, such as two heroes of one faction between Elijah and Lailah when the team has only those two, is reached only from a start near it, which is why there are 32 starts and not 16. Scores are kept by placement, so a placement two climbs reach is scored once.

Equally good placements are those with the best placement's `broken` and `goals` within the `margin` weight of the best. They are found by spreading step by step from the ones the climbs scored, until the count reaches 1,000, which the result reports as not complete. A better placement found on the way becomes the best and the spread starts again. The pick is the one with the highest total and threat points among them, then the lowest tiles in the units' order, and each hero keeps the tiles it stands on in any of them. Threat points are slow to read, since each enemy unit is tried on every enemy tile, so the placements are tried from the highest total down, and a placement's enemy rules are read heaviest first and stop once the rest could not lift it to the best so far. `full: true` scores every placement instead, which the tests compare the search with. `held` keeps the units it names on their tiles and moves only the others; the PvP team generator tries a swap that way (`swapPreview.ts`), the heroes swapped in placed around the units that stay where the board draws them, and reads a recorded placement with every unit held (`formation.ts`). Where freeing a recorded team's phantimal, alone or with one of its heroes, lets the phantimal's own goals (its Spirit Mark's) score higher, the generator draws the best such try, fewer units moved winning a tie, with every other unit on its recorded tile.

## Why a unit stands there

`explainPlacement` (`explain.ts`) gives the reasons for a placement `placeTeam` picked or held, which the PvP team generator shows in a card on each unit of a board, recorded or placed by the engine. A unit's reasons are the effects it gives or reaches, naming every ally a `target` goal's effect lands on and the heroes any other goal counts as met on, and those it receives where its goal counts it; the buff tile; the formation rules it keeps; and the side its hero leans to, left out when a rule says how far front or back it stands. Its free tiles that keep the placement as good (`asGoodAs`, as the search counts equally good) are drawn outlined. The enemy units' rules give no reasons, since they only choose among placements as good.

A reason names units by id and an effect by the data file its rule sits in: the hero's or phantimal's name, the charm's id or the artifact's name. The card words a `target`, `include` or `exactly` goal by `placement-gives-<file>` (`{heroes}`) and `placement-receives-<file>` (`{giver}`), and a `most` goal by `placement-reaches-<file>`, in `src/locales/app/placement/effects/`. A goal about enemy heroes gives no line. The rules test fails when a weighted rule has no words, or words name no rule.
